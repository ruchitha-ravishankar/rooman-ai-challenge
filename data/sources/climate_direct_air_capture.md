# Climate Direct Air Capture (DAC) and Basalt Mineralization

## Executive Summary
Direct Air Carbon Capture (DAC) systems remove ambient atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ (present at approximately 420 parts per million) through chemical absorption and physical adsorption. This dossier reviews thermodynamic limits, solid-sorbent vs liquid-solvent kinetics, and permanent in-situ basalt mineralization.

## 1. Solid Sorbent vs Liquid Solvent Thermodynamics
Solid sorbent DAC facilities utilize amine-functionalized porous silica or metal-organic framework (MOF) filters. Desorption operates via temperature-vacuum swing adsorption (TVSA) at relatively low regeneration temperatures ($80^\circ\text{C} - 100^\circ\text{C}$), requiring approximately 1.5 to 2.0 MWh of thermal energy plus 0.4 MWh of electrical energy per net metric ton of captured $\text{CO}_2$.
In contrast, liquid solvent systems employ aqueous potassium hydroxide ($\text{KOH}$) and calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) looping cycles requiring high-temperature calcination in a fluidized bed reactor at $900^\circ\text{C}$, drawing approximately 5.25 GJ of natural gas or electrical power per ton of $\text{CO}_2$.

## 2. In-Situ Basalt Mineralization and Storage Permanence
Captured $\text{CO}_2$ is dissolved in pressurized water (at approximately 25 bar) and injected into subterranean basaltic formations at depths exceeding 800 meters. The acidic carbonic solution dissolves divalent cations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Fe}^{2+}$) from basaltic rock, triggering spontaneous chemical precipitation of solid carbonate minerals (calcite $\text{CaCO}_3$, magnesite $\text{MgCO}_3$, and siderite $\text{FeCO}_3$). Radiocarbon isotopic tracing confirms that >95% of injected $\text{CO}_2$ is permanently mineralized into stone within 24 months, precluding any risk of seismic venting or leakage.

## 3. Levelized Cost of Carbon Removal
Current first-of-a-kind (FOAK) DAC plants operate at levelized costs between $600 and $850 per metric ton of $\text{CO}_2$. Projected nth-of-a-kind (NOAK) scaling through automated module manufacturing and renewable waste-heat integration targets $120 to $180 per ton by 2035.
