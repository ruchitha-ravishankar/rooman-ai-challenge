# Next-Generation CRISPR Gene Editing: Base Editing, Prime Editing, and In Vivo LNP Delivery

## Abstract
Precision genomic medicine has advanced beyond canonical Cas9 double-stranded breaks (DSBs) to single-nucleotide base editors and search-and-replace prime editing systems, substantially reducing genotoxic translocations and indel mutations.

## 1. Cytidine and Adenine Base Editors (CBE and ABE)
Base editors fuse a catalytically impaired Cas9 nickase (Cas9n D10A) to deaminase enzymes. Cytidine base editors (CBE) convert $\text{C}\cdot\text{G}$ to $\text{T}\cdot\text{A}$ base pairs using engineered cytidine deaminases (such as rAPOBEC1 or evoCDA1) within a 4-to-8 nucleotide target editing window. Adenine base editors (ABE8e) utilize laboratory-evolved deoxyadenosine deaminase TadA to mediate $\text{A}\cdot\text{T}$ to $\text{G}\cdot\text{C}$ transitions with >90% on-target editing efficiency and <0.1% unintended insertion/deletion frequencies.

## 2. Search-and-Replace Prime Editing Systems
Prime editing couples Cas9n to an engineered reverse transcriptase (M-MLV RT) guided by a prime editing guide RNA (pegRNA). The pegRNA specifies target locus binding and encodes the desired edit in an extension template. Prime editors install all 12 possible base-to-base transitions and transversions, targeted insertions up to 100 base pairs, and deletions up to 80 base pairs without requiring double-stranded DNA cleavage or donor repair templates.

## 3. Targeted In Vivo Lipid Nanoparticle (LNP) Delivery
In vivo hepatic and extrahepatic delivery utilizes ionizable lipid nanoparticles formulated with 50 mol% ionizable cationic lipid, 38.5 mol% cholesterol, 10 mol% DSPC, and 1.5 mol% PEG-lipid. Incorporation of organ-specific targeting ligands (such as GalNAc for hepatocytes or anti-CD4 antibodies for T-cells) achieves >85% organ accumulation with single-dose therapeutic target knockdown in clinical trials for transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR).
