# Magnetic Confinement Fusion: High-Temperature Superconductors and SPARC Tokamaks

## Abstract
Recent advances in Rare-Earth Barium Copper Oxide (REBCO) high-temperature superconducting (HTS) magnets enable magnetic fields exceeding 20 Tesla on axis, dramatically reducing the physical volume required for net energy gain in compact tokamak reactors.

## 1. High-Temperature Superconducting (HTS) Magnet Confinement
The fusion power density in a magnetic confinement tokamak scales with the fourth power of the magnetic field:
$$P_{fusion} \propto \beta^2 B^4$$
Traditional low-temperature superconducting (LTS) magnets (Niobium-Tin $\text{Nb}_3\text{Sn}$) are limited to magnetic fields of 12 to 13 Tesla and require liquid helium cooling at $4.2\text{ K}$. Modern REBCO HTS tape operates stably at $20\text{ K}$ under on-coil fields of 20.1 Tesla. This allows the SPARC tokamak (major radius $R_0 = 1.85\text{ m}$) to achieve the same plasma confinement capability as the ITER facility ($R_0 = 6.2\text{ m}$) in less than one-fortieth of the volume.

## 2. Plasma Heating and Q-Breakeven Performance Metrics
SPARC is engineered to operate in high-confinement mode (H-mode) utilizing Deuterium-Tritium (D-T) fuel mixtures. Auxiliary ion cyclotron resonance heating (ICRH) injects 25 MW of radiofrequency power to heat the central ion core above $150\text{ million }^\circ\text{C}$ (13 keV). The projected fusion energy gain factor $Q = P_{fusion} / P_{in}$ exceeds $Q \ge 11$, producing over 140 MW of net fusion power during 10-second pulses.

## 3. Liquid Metal Divertors and Tritium Breeding Blankets
Divertor strike plates encounter peak parallel heat fluxes exceeding $100\text{ MW/m}^2$. Flowing liquid lithium and tin-lithium divertor targets withstand these thermal loads without macroscopic surface erosion. Surrounding lithium-lead ($\text{Pb-17Li}$) breeding blankets capture 14.1 MeV fusion neutrons, generating tritium through neutron capture reactions on Lithium-6 ($^6\text{Li} + n \rightarrow ^4\text{He} + ^3\text{H} + 4.8\text{ MeV}$) with a Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR) of 1.15.
